Winless No More: Dreyer and Sisniega End SDFC's 10-Game Drought at St. Louis

St. Louis CITY SC 0–1 San Diego FC  ·  Saturday, October 31, 2026, 12:00 PM PT  ·  Energizer Park, St. Louis

Three days after the season's hope officially ended in Vancouver, San Diego FC found a different kind of ending in St. Louis: a win. A 1–0 victory at Energizer Park on Saturday snapped a ten-match winless run, gave the club its first clean sheet hero of the season, and sent SDFC into the home finale with something they had not had in weeks — a good feeling.

It was not comfortable. It was not pretty for long stretches. St. Louis outshot San Diego 16–12 and put seven efforts on target to SDFC's three. But football games are not decided on shot counts. They are decided on one left-footed strike, six saves, and a clearance off the line in stoppage time.

Dreyer, from the left half-space

The goal came in the 39th minute, and it was a captain's kind of goal — not in the armband sense, but in the sense that someone had to decide this game was winnable. Anders Dreyer collected the ball in the left half-space, outside the box, with Alvarado and Ingvartsen involved in the build-up, and curled a left-footed effort home. It was his team-leading goal of the season, and it was enough.

That is worth pausing on. Dreyer had missed a penalty a week earlier against Seattle — saved, with Bakambu burying the rebound. Players carry those moments. Dreyer answered his with the only goal of a game his team desperately needed to win, away from home, against the third-best team in the West.

Sisniega's afternoon

If Dreyer won the game, Pablo Sisniega kept it won. The goalkeeper made six saves in his fifth start of the season — and kept his first clean sheet of the year, the club's eighth. Every one of those saves mattered more as the second half wore on and St. Louis, chasing a fourth straight defeat after a record-tying 19-match unbeaten run, threw everything forward.

The defining defensive moment, though, belonged to someone else. Deep into stoppage time, with St. Louis pressing for an equalizer, Manu Duah cleared Becher's effort off the goal line. One inch the other way and the winless run reaches eleven, the story is heartbreak again, and the finale week starts with a sigh. Duah made sure it did not.

What the win actually changes

The record. SDFC move to 9-13-11, 38 points, still 13th in the West — the table does not flatter them, and it should not. The playoffs are gone; Wednesday settled that. But a winless run is a weight, and it is off now. Players stop answering questions about the drought. The training ground gets lighter. Small things, but real ones, in a long season.

The opponent. Do not undersell this result. St. Louis came into the game third in the West at 14-10-9 and 51 points, a team that had gone unbeaten for 19 straight league games between May and October and had just lifted the U.S. Open Cup on penalties at Columbus on October 21. Their fourth straight loss is their problem. Beating them, in their building, with a rotated goalkeeper and a backs-to-the-wall second half, is a genuine result for a team with nothing left to play for but pride.

The finale. All of this funnels into one place: Snapdragon Stadium, Saturday, November 7, 4:00 PM PT, against Sporting Kansas City on Decision Day. The home finale. A season that began with a conference final and ends with a 13th-place fight still deserves a full house and a proper goodbye — and now, for the first time in ten games, the team walking out will be walking out as winners. St. Louis close their season at Houston the same day. San Diego close theirs at home, where it started, where the fans who never left deserve one more night.

Winless no more. See you at Snapdragon for the finale.
